# FireAtlas methodology and validation plan

Status on November 14, 2026. The pilot dashboard exports use locally fitted count factors for Terra and NOAA-20. The results cover Bulgaria, South Africa and Canada. See [pilot results](PROGRESS.md#pilot-dashboard-export).

## Measurement and sensor differences

FIRMS rows are positive thermal-anomaly detections, located at the center of a flagged pixel. Multiple rows can come from one fire, and a pixel can contain multiple fires or a non-fire heat source. The recorded `scan` and `track` dimensions describe pixel footprint, whose area changes with view angle. Pixel area cannot be interpreted as area burned. FRP represents radiative power at observation time and is excluded from the first index. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq)

MODIS has roughly 1 km nadir fire pixels. VIIRS has nominal 375 m fire pixels and responds more readily to small fires, with improved nighttime sensitivity. Satellite swath width and polar orbit overlap change the number of looks with latitude. Historically Terra crossed at about 10:30 and 22:30 mean local time, Aqua and S-NPP at about 13:30 and 01:30, and NOAA-20 sampled earlier than S-NPP. Terra/Aqua orbit drift changes the historical times. Confidence semantics and detection algorithms differ, so a common numeric confidence threshold is inappropriate. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) · [MODIS fire guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) · [VIIRS fire guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) · [NASA Earthdata orbit timing discussion](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-04/MCDWD_VCDWD_UserGuide_RevE_04.22.25.pdf)

## Bounded experiment

1. Stream files listed for each country. Normalize UTC dates and platform identifiers from the row and manifest. Count raw rows before filtering. For the primary experiment use presumed vegetation fires where the documented `type=0` field is available. Mark unknown type and test sensitivity to including it. Start with all documented confidence classes and repeat with the nominal/high classes defined separately in the product guides. Log row counts lost to every filter.
2. Aggregate by country, platform and UTC calendar month. Record file presence, platform availability, known missing days and partial mission months separately from detected counts. A missing file, inactive platform or unusable period produces an unavailable value with a reason. A present and eligible product period with no catalog detections can report a count of zero alongside unknown actual observation coverage. Flag months with known gaps and exclude them from calibration and scoring for the affected platform pair. Preserve them in the inspection view.
3. Establish an uncalibrated monthly hotspot count baseline against a fixed Aqua MODIS reference, chosen because its historical nominal overpass was close to S-NPP's. Compare with (a) a platform-specific count multiplier estimated only over paired training months and (b) a common equal-area grid with a starting resolution of 10 km. In (b), count distinct cells with detections per platform and UTC day, sum cell-days in the month, and fit a separate platform multiplier against Aqua cell-days. Compare both the uncalibrated and calibrated grid metrics on the same grid target. Never compare errors across different targets without a common scale. For each target, start with a positive multiplier equal to the ratio of summed reference and source activity on eligible training months. Publish sample sizes and suppress an estimate when overlap or activity is too sparse.
4. To validate, use 2013–2021 for development (NOAA-20 from 2019), 2022–2023 to choose the method and 2024–2025 as the untouched final holdout. Show paired sample counts, MAE, signed bias and rank correlation by country/platform. Use both absolute and relative error summaries with the reference scale shown and relative errors reported only for positive reference months. Check high-season month ranks, day/night, product-version changes and errors near platform transitions. Use Bulgaria, South Africa and Canada as a minimum; include Brazil if benchmarking allows. Check geographic transfer by fitting pooled ratios without one country and evaluating on that country. Country-specific coefficients may be published only where paired history supports them.
5. Prefer the simplest candidate that beats its baseline on median per-country error in development validation and has acceptable bias and seasonal ranking on the final holdout. As a planning gate, aim for at least 10% median MAE improvement and record all country-level regressions. Freeze method and thresholds before reviewing the final holdout. If the holdout fails, show the candidate as experimental and mark unsupported periods instead of claiming a comparable record.

## Historical series and coverage

Use one platform per country-month for the index, with predetermined era boundaries and visible source labels. Initial candidate uses Terra bridged to Aqua for 2000–2002, Aqua for 2003–2018, and bridged NOAA-20 from 2019. Retain S-NPP as an independent overlap diagnostic and possible fixed VIIRS-era substitute only if selected and validated before holdout. Keep Terra, Aqua, S-NPP and NOAA-20 in the raw comparison. Use a validated bridge only in supported months and countries. Check training-era factors against the oldest available pairing and describe the extrapolation risk from orbit drift and MODIS collection changes. Avoid choosing a platform because it happens to report more detections that month.

Display Aqua-equivalent detected hotspots for the count method or Aqua-equivalent detected cell-days for the grid method, with an optional same-calendar-month historical median and ratio when the denominator and history support it. Carry the unit and platform in the UI. A cell-day is a measure of spatial and temporal extent of *detected activity*. Multiple days may reflect the same incident. Within-country comparisons are the initial use case.

The pilot exporter uses the same-era Terra bridge fitted on 2003–2008 and evaluated on 2009–2011, and the local NOAA-20 bridge fitted on 2019–2021 and evaluated on 2024–2025. For this provisional export a bridge needs at least 24 training pairs, 12 evaluation pairs, and at least 10% improvement in mean absolute error over unadjusted counts. Terra estimates for 2001–2002 are labeled extrapolations. The partial 2000 start is available via raw detections. Months flagged as affected by a known outage on the selected index platform have null index values. The Sofia pilot retains the main experiment's all-confidence filter.

Paired sensor agreement measures consistency with the reference. Reference detections also have omissions and false positives. To estimate observation opportunity or correct for cloud and swath gaps, obtain per-platform swath or gridded fire masks with valid clear and non-fire pixels, geolocation and overpass times, QA, missing granules and view geometry. Use covered clear land area or valid cell/passes as the denominator, and account for repeated looks. The hotspot CSVs and mission-wide missing-day list cannot provide this denominator. The MVP uses a historical indicator for exploration with a 2025 cutoff. [FIRMS FAQ on standard products and QA](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) · [FIRMS missing-data API](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/api/missing_data/)



## Dashboard comparison design

Compare the selected platform's recorded vegetation detections with the Aqua-equivalent estimate for the same country and month. Expose known outages, missing records and unsupported estimates explicitly. The map shows recorded hotspot coordinates. Use held-out agreement with Aqua to assess the adjustment, with early Terra extrapolation still labeled.
